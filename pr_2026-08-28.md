One squashed commit: 44 files, +5426 / −542.

## converge — where two definitions about the same thing stop agreeing

Every other pass answers *are these two the same*. This one reports the step they part on.

```console
$ find-dup-defs ./src --converge
--- divergences in functions (converge — one thing done twice, and where they part) ---
DUPLICATE FUNCTION [INFO]: _apply_source_rate_limit / _enforce_submit_budget
  votes[4]: text×0 shape×62 subject×6 fork×12
  A only: _v2 = utcnow()
  B only: _v1 = now(UTC)
  ~ _ = _ - _(_=_)
    A: _v3 = _v2 - timedelta(hours=1)
    B: _v2 = _v1 - timedelta(seconds=HOUR_SECONDS)
  subject: domain.rate_limit.repo (reached by 11 definitions)
```

`text×0` — those two share **no line**. One rate limiter written four times across a codebase, and
the four disagree about where "now" comes from.

### Two anchors, because one is structurally blind

A pass keyed on a **shared statement** can only see divergence that grew out of textual agreement:
a copy that drifted, or two paths that converged. It cannot reach the opposite case — two places
written independently about one thing, with no line in common. Measured, that blind spot is real: a
pair of functions answering one question ("does this channel fit the plan") shared exactly one name
between them, and that name was `int`.

What such a pair does share is a **subject**: both reach the same module. Imports are the corpus's
own declaration of what a definition is about, so the frontend resolves the dotted path each name it
uses stands for, and the engine takes prefixes of it — which is how *imported the module* and
*imported a member of it* are made to meet, instead of failing to meet as strings.

| anchor | agreement | divergence | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **statement** | same words | different names | one decision made in two ways |
| **subject** | same entity, same shape | different words | one procedure written twice |

### One currency

The seed decides only what the report points at, never how a pair is weighed:

```text
score = (E_text + E_shape + E_subject) · D · sharpness · novelty / members
```

**E** is how surprising the coincidence is, in nats, over the three ways two definitions can
evidently be one thing done twice. **D** is the rarity of the rarest name they *part* on. **novelty**
is `1` when they found each other again after the gap and `1 − jaccard` when they parted for good —
for a drifted copy alikeness is the premise, for a permanent fork it means a similarity pass already
has the pair.

**members** is the one factor measured rather than assumed: read against real code, a divergence
between exactly two places was worth acting on 74% of the time and one among three or more 16%.

### Families

Many places around one subject, all one shape, said **once**:

```console
--- families of functions (converge — many places, one subject, one shape) ---
DUPLICATE FUNCTION [INFO]: _claimable_tasks_family / _blocked_tasks_family /
                           _oldest_claimable_family / _oldest_blocked_family
  votes[2]: shape×30 members×4   # 4 definitions around prometheus_client.core.GaugeMetricFamily
```

A group of N definitions produces N(N−1)/2 pairs, and reporting those says one fact once per pair
while burying it. In a read of fifty findings on a real tree, four slots went to pairs among one
family of six sibling functions and three more to a set of chart builders — the finding was never
"these two are alike", it was "this family exists".

Greedy maximal cliques, not connected components: under single-linkage `1-2-3-4` is one group whose
ends share no edge. The clique proposes and the evidence disposes — members are dropped while
dropping raises the score, so a clique that grew past the real family shrinks back to it, with no
threshold and no notion of which member is the odd one.

### Advisory, and capped

Findings are **INFO** and never gate. `--converge-top` keeps the strongest 50 of each kind by
default, and this is the one pass capped where no other is. The others report a **set**: a cluster
either is a duplicate or is not, and cutting off would drop findings as true as the ones kept. This
reports a **ranking** with no threshold anywhere in it, where the tail is what the ordering exists to
push away. Uncapped it emitted 198k lines on a mid-sized tree. `--converge-top 0` still prints all.

## The contract that makes it language-blind

`Def::facets` — one struct, not loose fields, because a frontend author needs a single place saying
what it must answer. Split across two, a language implements one perspective pass and silently not
the other, which is exactly how things got here.

| facet | what | why it cannot be derived later |
|---|---|---|
| `statements` | every statement at every nesting level, header first at depth 0 | flattened, `for x in xs: / f() / g()` is indistinguishable from the three statements where `g()` runs *after* the loop — only the walk that produced them knows |
| `reaches` | the dotted path each imported name it uses stands for, separator normalized to `.` | the corpus's own declaration of what a definition is *about* |

Deliberately **not** `type3_lines` with depths bolted on, though on Python the two coincide: Rust
emits one line per top-level block statement with a nested `if` inlined whole, so tying them together
would either change an established pass's behaviour or leave the other languages nothing to report.

Both are empty when a frontend does not report them, and every pass reading them self-gates on that
— a language lights up by filling them in, with no engine edit and no list of supported languages
anywhere.

`AnalyzedFn` stops being a bare 4-tuple. That anonymity is why the statement stream did not exist: a
fifth slot in a tuple reads as noise at every destructuring site, so the block structure the walk
already knew was thrown away.

## lenses on three languages

The ten questions were never Python-specific; the machinery around them was. Moved into the core, a
frontend contributes an AST walk and nothing else. Three answers were worth thinking about rather
than transliterating:

- **What Rust holds open.** Rust has no `with`, and the temptation is to leave `resources` empty. Its
  real answer is a **guard** — a binding whose value is never read again and whose only job is to
  live until the scope ends. Structurally detectable, so no list of blessed names; the same call
  bound to a name that *is* read is not a guard, and the test says so.
- **What TypeScript holds open.** `using` / `await using`, not `try`/`finally`. A `finally` is a
  cleanup *path*, projected as control flow; reading every one as a held resource would fill the lens
  with the language's commonest idiom.
- **How Rust fails.** `Err(MyError::Empty)` keeps the path whole: the enum is the failure family and
  the variant the specific failure.

## Two bugs the dogfood found that no plan listed

**The fork term was measuring grammar.** Python's canonical is source-like; the Rust and TypeScript
canonicals are s-expr dumps where identifiers live inside the quotes and the bare words are node
tags. Read with one rule, Rust parted on `Path` / `Method` / `Bind` / `Ref` and Python on `if` /
`return` / `None` / `await` — half the score was the language. `CanonDialect` is the contract's own
mechanism for this and now drives it. Rust and TypeScript now part on `demote_one`/`kick_one` and
`seat_ceiling`/`row_ceiling`; Python's fork vocabulary went to `clientspace_id`/`channel_id`.

**A relative import named that it was relative.** `./a/b` recorded as written gave every relative
import in a tree a bare `.` prefix node, and eighteen of a hundred and seventy-five frontend families
formed on that alone. Resolved against the importing file's directory. Which surfaced a second one a
level up — families around "these files are in the same repository" — because the family index,
built separately, had not inherited the pair index's cap on a subject everything reaches.

## Cleanup this made possible, and one it made necessary

**Four experiment binaries deleted.** `cargo package` was shipping them inside `py-canon`, including
a fork of a pass in the same release. With them went `statement_streams` / `DefStream`, the public
`module_imports`, and the unparser's whole module-alias machinery — reachable only through a flag
those examples passed. −3553 lines.

**`find-dup-defs-canon` folded into `dup-defs-core`.** The split existed so the engine would not pull
in frontend implementation; the perspective passes ended that in both directions — the engine reads
`reach::prefixes`, and the lens machinery needs `Def` itself. A boundary with holes on both sides is
a version to bump and a publish order to get right for nothing.

**The self-gate is green again.** `find-dup-defs crates -D @find-dup-defs.directives` had been red
before this branch: twelve of the errors were the golden fixtures, which are duplicated on purpose
and exist to be found. Suppressed now — an advisory finding about a file whose whole job is to
contain the finding is not advice. The ten this branch adds are the same irreducibility the
directives file already documents, one rung up: three lens walks and two statement walkers, each
answering the same questions from Ruff, syn or oxc nodes. De-escalated, not hidden.

## Verified rather than assumed

- Default run, `--kinds lenses` and `--patternology` are **byte-identical** before and after, on all
  three languages, compared against a binary built from the pre-branch commit and run from one
  working directory.
- New goldens lock converge and lenses, on their own fixture — teaching `mixed` to exercise converge
  would move every existing golden for passes it has nothing to do with.
- Output is **deterministic** across runs. Four sources of nondeterminism were found by running the
  same tree twice and diffing, the last of them float summation over a `HashMap`, where addition's
  non-associativity moved the last bits and reordered equally-scoring pairs. A ranking whose ties are
  decided by the hasher is not reproducible, and reproducibility is the difference between a finding
  and a coincidence.
- `cargo test --release` and `cargo clippy --release --all-targets` — what CI runs — are green:
  **137 tests**, no warnings.
- `cargo publish -p dup-defs-core --dry-run` succeeds; the frontends' dry-runs will fail until the
  core is actually published, which is expected for a `path` + `version` chain.
- No wall-time regression on the default path: py 0.15 s, ts 0.09 s, rs 0.04 s, unchanged.

## Versions

Breaking on 0.x, so the minor moves. Publish order is core, then the three frontends, then the
engine — the frontends pin `dup-defs-core = "0.2"`.

| crate | | why |
|---|---|---|
| `dup-defs-core` | 0.1.4 → **0.2.0** | `Def` gained `facets`; `AnalyzedFn` tuple → struct; absorbed `find-dup-defs-canon` |
| `py-canon` | 0.3.2 → **0.4.0** | `statement_streams` / `DefStream` / `Import` / `module_imports` off the public surface; `Lens` moved to the core |
| `rs-canon` | 0.1.1 → **0.2.0** | re-exports the changed `AnalyzedFn`; new opt-in `lenses` kind |
| `ts-canon` | 0.1.4 → **0.2.0** | same |
| `find-dup-defs` | 0.8.0 → **0.9.0** | `--converge`, `--converge-top`, lenses on three languages |

`find-dup-defs-canon` is not bumped because it no longer exists; consumers pinned to 0.1 keep
resolving the last published copy. The PyPI wheel version follows `find-dup-defs` automatically
(`dynamic = ["version"]`).

## Known debt, not blocking

- **LGG is still implemented twice** — Plotkin over `patternology`'s AST-dump term trees and over
  `converge`'s flat token sequences. Different granularities, one theory.
- On framework-heavy corpora both anchors fire on the **framework**: `react.useState` is the shared
  subject and the shared shape at once. The cap on a subject everything reaches trims this; it does
  not cure it.
